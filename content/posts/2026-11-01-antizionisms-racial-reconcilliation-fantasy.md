My partner and I are child-free lesbians; me in my late thirties, she in her mid fourties. It's not my entire identity, but it comes up sometimes.

It comes up as I slowly slide into middle age, noticing that I can no longer think to myself "wow, isn't that a bit young?" when old classmates announce their pregnancy. It comes up when my body, which doesn't care about my lacking desire or my gender identity, tells me it's time. It comes up when I see — and I do everywhere — that my life stage is the one when people are busy raising children. 

---

In becoming close friends with a parent, I've come to realise non-parents forget what parenthood requires: a kind-hearted, well-natured 





I consider it a privilege to be child-free and to be experiencing a very actively ticking clock from time to time. I think I do because I've been developing a growing fondness for the signals my body gives me. 

It wasn't always like this.

It's a conscious decision of me not to raise children, because I feel I have too much catching up to do. I think if I ignored that task and did my best to conceive, carry, birth, and raise a child, I'd be repeating a pattern that I'm sorry my parents developed. 



It makes me feel sorry for women with such a clock who want children very badly but who don't find them in optimal circumstances.
