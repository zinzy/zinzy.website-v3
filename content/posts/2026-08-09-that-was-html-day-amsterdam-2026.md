Yesterday, at the headquarters of [Internet Archive Europe](https://www.internetarchive.eu/), I hosted the [second Amsterdam edition of HTML Day](/2026/06/27/html-day-2026-amsterdam-edition/)! The [first edition](https://bobs-club.net/html-day-2025-08-02/index.html) was hosted last year by [Braxton Hall](https://braxtonhall.ca/), who I was pleased joined us yesterday for HTML fun.

I figured three or four people show up, and I would host it at a park somewhere. Instead, [Holly Zijderveld](https://holly.garden/) reached out to suggest we use the IAE venue right in the centre of Amsterdam. It was a very sunny day, and still 13 people gathered to work on HTML pages, talk about independent web publishing, and everything else that came floating by.

### My takeaways

- It is refreshing to write vanilla HTML. I came to HTML Day thinking about my uncle Peter. When I first began making websites age 11, I one day asked him a question about building software. He said to me: ["you don't _build_ software, you _write_ it."](https://sites.elliott.computer/html-day-august-8-2026/) I wanted to honour that by writing vanilla HTML without autocompletion, on my iPad. It was mildly embarrassing, cumbersome, and ultimately very good for my nervous system.
- I thoroughly enjoyed talking Indieweb shop with fellow enthusiasts!
- I explicitly told my coworkers not to show up thinking their technical understanding would overwhelm HTML newbies. It turned out, every person at the table was an IT professional. Sorry Panos!
- I loved how diverse the group of attendees was; in ethnicity, gender and sexual identity, and style of website making.
